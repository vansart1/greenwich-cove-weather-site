The Sound is running a strong buffer today. Long Island Sound is 74.7°F against 86°F inland air — an 11.3°F gap. Because water holds its temperature while land heats up and then cools off, that gap is not a fixed number: it stays wide all afternoon, and only closes below the level that matters at about 8:52 PM, once the evening air has cooled toward the water temperature. For this 1–5 PM window the layer is at full strength the entire time.

What that means for the cove. A wide gap puts a stable layer of air over the water that starves the low levels of an approaching storm. Today's cells sit to the west and west-northwest, so anything that did turn back toward us would cross the Sound on the way in and run into that layer — the geometry that gives the cove its biggest advantage. Expect the cove to see wind without the rain and thunder that inland Fairfield and Westchester get from the same cell.

What would change the call. Two things. If instability climbs past about 3,000 J/kg, a storm can punch straight through the stable layer instead of being choked by it — worth watching, though today's 1,870 is comfortably short of that. And after roughly 8:52 PM the buffer is gone: a cell arriving late this evening should be taken at face value, with no discount for the Sound.
